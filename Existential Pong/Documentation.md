## * My interpretation of Existentialism * ##

Existential thoughts about life's meaning and truth has been a recurring notions in my everyday life. I chose existentialism to understand more about life and purpose of life. The world seemed absurd in many ways to me. It's always been my pursuit to understand the world. I realized that if i wanted to understand the world, first i needed to understand myself. Introspective thoughts creeped in everytime i did something or thought about something. I've always tried to understand and analyze where my thoughts and perspectives come from. I think about if I'm making any assumptions and try to think and see different perspectives. When i was not able to express my feelings and thoughts, I tried to capture these thoughts through expressive digital artworks as a means of relief from these terrifying existential thoughts. Anyways...

Existentialism is a philosophical concept in which person starts thinking about the essence of their existence. This school of thought was created by the German Philosopher Frederich Nietzche.

Is meaning and purpose pre determined or do we determine our meaning and purpose. To take up the terrifying responsibility of self-creation is where the heart of Existentialism lies.

Its a thought process which emphasizes individual existence, freedom, and choice, asserting that people must create their own meaning in a world without inherent purpose or preordained essence. This philosophy explores the human condition, stressing that individuals are responsible for their actions and the values they create through their free will, leading to a profound sense of freedom and responsibility that can also cause anguish and anxiety.

#### The Will to power 
#### The Will to live
#### The Will to not exist 

The Will to Power resembles the choice to make your own meaning, your own purpose when the world or existence loses meaning or when you lose your purpose, either by natural causes or by your own cause. You start to realize this existence without meaning is absurd.
Thus, you chose to create your own meaning, your own purpose, you choose Progress, You choose transcendence.   
The instinct to grow, overcome, and affirm life in the face of suffering and chaos. The individual fulfills their freedom by exercising this will, imposing their values and direction onto the indifferent world.           
You become The Übermensch, the Overman.
You achieve True/Radical Freedom. You become a person who dictates their own fate.


The Will to Live resembles the choice to adhere to your given role or purpose. Just keep doing what your surroundings wants of you. It's living life as you were given and doing nothing more or less. The Last Man shuns difficulty, avoids self-examination, seeks comfortable mediocrity, and accepts inherited or popular values. The individual denies their freedom and hides behind predetermined roles or social norms.


The Will to not Exist is fundamentally not a existentialist thought rather a nihilistic thought. An inner drive for retreat, stagnation, and negation; a rejection of life's painful but necessary struggles. An easy way out of life's misery and pain.

## Designing the GamePlay experience  ##

The experience i wanted to create is, more than being a fun or joking take on existentialism, i wanted the player to get a glimpse of true existentialism. 
An experience where the player will not know what to do next, like getting stuck in existential crisis. An experience where the player has to think existential questions and answer these questions themselves to bring themselves out of the existential crisis phase. Either by exercising their will and values or by removing themselves from the equation. The idea was to create a surreal playable experience, leaving the player with new perspectives about life. 

The game is intentionally secretive about the input needed to progress, leaving room exploration for the player. In order to progress, the player to experiment with a combination of arrow keys inputs and multi-key sequences.
I wanted to take advantage of something only the medium of games posses, PLAYER AGENCY. Unlike the opponent, an automated paddle doing what it is programmed to do, the player has agency to alter the rules of the game and exercise free will.

## Role of Music and Songs in the Gameplay ##

The music and songs are carefully thought out decisions and fundamentally ties to the existential themes.

### Radiohead's "Everything in Its Right Place" ###

<audio controls src="assets/song_1.mp3" title="Title"></audio>

Radiohead's "Everything in Its Right Place" is used with powerful irony. The simple, hypnotic, and superficially calm repetition of the phrase and the track's driving rhythm serve as the soundtrack to the STAGNATION state. This music represents the player's complacent acceptance of the rigid, predetermined rules of the system: the score limit, the fixed paddle size, and the mandated vertical movement. When the player ultimately reaches the score limit, they are deemed The Last Man—not because they achieved a great victory, but because they comfortably embraced the illusion that the game's rules were sufficient and that their existence was indeed "in its right place," thereby avoiding the painful yet necessary freedom of self-creation.

This song has been on repeat for the longest time ever since i came across it in the Game/Playable experience Kid-A-mnesia. A song so eerie, so mysterious yet giving a sense of calm.

### Radiohead's "Kid A" ###

<audio controls src="assets/song_2.mp3" title="Title"></audio>

Breaking the Mold (The Absurd): When the player moves the paddle horizontally or activates a multi-key sequence, they reject the game's original purpose (scoring) and its fixed rules (vertical movement). The shift to the cold, unsettling sound of "Kid A" immediately signals that the player has entered a realm beyond the comforting, if deceptive, order of STAGNATION. This is the sound of the Absurd—a world stripped bare of inherent meaning.

Loss of Identity (The Alienation): The song's processed, robotic, and unrecognizable vocals reflect the player's intentional disintegration of the self as defined by the system. The player is no longer the "Pong player" serving the rules; they are an anonymous, free entity wrestling with the new, chaotic power of self-modification. The music reinforces the realization that there is no "true self" or inherited purpose to fall back on—only the raw, disorienting experience of radical freedom.

"Kid A" acts as the base frequency of the new reality. It is the sound of possibility, but also of dread. It is the sound of being trapped in state of existential crisis.


### Frank Sinatra's "My Way" ###

<audio controls src="assets/song_3.mp3" title="Title"></audio>

Frank Sinatra's "My Way" is an iconic song that serves as a powerful, retrospective declaration of a life lived on one's own terms.
The core meaning Sinatra and the lyricist (Paul Anka) sought to convey is self-affirmation, individualism, and defiance in the face of judgment.

Retrospective Justification
The song is structured as a final, reflective summary of a life already lived. The narrator is facing their end ("The final curtain") but finds solace not in divine judgment or external approval, but in the internal conviction that they were true to themselves.

The fundamental message is that authenticity is the ultimate measure of a life's worth. Mistakes were made, but they were my mistakes, leading to the phrase: "The record shows I took the blows—and did it my way."


In Game, As soon as the player's paddle is close to covering the whole screen, this song is introduced, the swing and brass of "My Way" would declare their radical freedom not as an existential burden, but as a personal triumph. It immediately places the player in the role of the ultimate Individualist, celebrating the break from the system's "Last Man" stagnation.

The Übermensch: The song is the definitive anthem of success, self-determination, and the retrospective validation of one's choices. In this phase, the player is exercising their Will to Power by literally changing the rules of their reality (the paddle's dimensions). The song serves as a boast that every unconventional input, every horizontal shift, and every act of defiance against the Pong system is an act chosen and owned by the player—"I did it my way."

Validation of Self-Creation: The song acts as the self-generated memento of the player's choices. Everything they did—moving horizontally, holding complex key sequences, expanding their form—led to this moment. The grandeur of the orchestra validates the player’s self-imposed purpose, declaring that their absurd acts of creation have successfully overcome the universe's meaninglessness.


### Radiohead's "How to disappear completely" ###

<audio controls src="assets/song_4.mp3" title="Title"></audio>

The song was written by Thom Yorke during a particularly agonizing period of the band's career following the success of OK Computer. Yorke experienced extreme anxiety, touring exhaustion, and the profound discomfort of being a reluctant public figure.

The repeated line, "I'm not here / This isn't happening," is not poetic nihilism; it's a direct transcription of a coping mechanism Yorke used. When overwhelmed, he would mentally repeat this phrase to detach himself from reality, a psychological defense against overwhelming stress. The song is thus about disassociation—the feeling of losing connection to one's thoughts, feelings, and sense of identity.

In the game, The song’s expansive, sorrowful, yet strangely calming orchestral arrangement, creates a feeling of overwhelming vastness and inevitable surrender. As the paddle shrinks to near non-existence from the player choice of inputs, the music sonically engulfs the player, conveying the feeling of the self dissolving into an infinite void. This isn't a quick plunge into nihilism; it is a profound, melancholic farewell to the world.

The lyrical core of the song—"I'm not here / This isn't happening"—perfectly mirrors the player's mental state as they pursue self-destruction. The player, in their pursuit of Ultimate Freedom, is actively choosing to deny the reality of the game, the importance of the ball, and the meaning of their actions. The music becomes the internal monologue of the character detaching from their senses, memories, and physical form.

Unlike the aggressive declaration of "My Way," this music is an acceptance. It signals the end of struggle and the final surrender to the belief that existence itself is pointless. The shrinking paddle is a visible act of giving up agency, culminating in the serene, devastating resolution of becoming The Void. The song validates this choice, portraying it not as a failure, but as a quiet, irreversible philosophical conclusion.
